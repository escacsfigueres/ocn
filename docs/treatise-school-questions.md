# Questions to put to a source, one head at a time

Companion to [`treatise-school-worksheet.md`](treatise-school-worksheet.md).
The worksheet says which fifteen openings need attributing; this says
what to ask about each, in a form you can paste into a research
assistant with the literature loaded, or take to a library.

## Why the questions are shaped this way

Four things have to come back before a claim can be written, and asking
for them separately is what stops a plausible answer from becoming a
citation. This project has already retracted one source that sounded
right and pointed at the wrong book.

1. **Does a reference work confirm the eponym at all?** Not "is it
   commonly called that" -- who says so, in print.
2. **Which work, edition and page?** An edition matters: Allgaier's
   analysis of his gambit is in the fourth edition of 1819, not the
   first of 1795, and citing the wrong one is citing a book that does
   not contain the claim.
3. **What was the person's actual role?** This is the question most
   often skipped and most often wrong. `analysed-in` is not
   `invented-by`. The Ruy López carries its author's name because he
   wrote the book, not because he invented 3.Bb5, which was played
   before him.
4. **Is the attribution disputed?** A contested name is a finding, not a
   problem: the Allgaier row already records that the Oxford Companion
   notes the line was played by Cotter around 1780 and is sometimes
   named for him.

An answer that cannot produce (2) is `attested` at best. An answer that
produces all four from a reference work is `verified`.

## The template

> For the opening known as **{name}**, attributed to **{person}**:
> 1. Which reference work states that the opening is named after this
>    person? Give the work, edition and page or entry.
> 2. In which of their own publications does the analysis appear? Give
>    the title, the edition and the year of that edition.
> 3. Was this person the inventor of the line, the first to publish an
>    analysis of it, or the populariser of a line already played? Quote
>    the passage that decides it.
> 4. Does any source dispute the attribution or credit someone else?
>
> If a point cannot be established from the sources available, say so
> rather than inferring it.

That last line is not politeness. An assistant asked to be helpful will
fill a gap with something plausible, and a plausible gap-filler is
exactly the failure this list exists to prevent.

## What to do with the answers

Paste them back and they become a manifest: one row per head, the
relation chosen from the closed set (`invented-by`, `analysed-in`,
`popularised-by`, `named-after-person`), the source recorded with its
edition, and the grade set by what the answer could actually support. It
gets a dry-run and then a GO, like every other lot.

Heads that come back thin stay unattributed. An empty field is a
question still open; a wrong field is a lie with a citation attached.
