# The treatise school: a research worksheet

**Status: candidates, not claims. Nothing is applied and no manifest
exists.** This is the shortlist the attribution work starts from, sized
and sourced so the registrar can decide row by row. Step 2 of
[`chronicle-layer-design.md`](chronicle-layer-design.md), after the
world championship map.

## Why this lot first

The openings named for the men who wrote the founding treatises are the
best possible opening move for a graded attribution programme, for three
reasons that rarely coincide:

- **The sources are reference-grade and free.** Ruy López's *Libro de la
  invención liberal* (1561), Philidor's *L'analyse des échecs* (1749),
  Ponziani (1769), Allgaier (1795) and the rest are public domain and
  scanned. A reader can check the claim without a subscription, which is
  the bar this project set itself.
- **The claims are uncontroversial.** That the Ruy López is named for
  Ruy López de Segura is not a naming dispute; it is the settled
  account. The work here is citation, not adjudication.
- **The relation is the interesting one.** These are `analysed-in`
  claims -- an opening named for the author who published its analysis
  -- which is the relation that makes "Greco looked at this in his
  manuscripts" a queryable fact rather than a sentence in prose.

## Scale

842 catalogue rows carry one of these names. That number is misleading
and worth stating plainly: the claim belongs on the **head** of each
family, and the 307 rows under `C.RyL` inherit it through the parent
chain rather than each needing a citation of their own. The lot is
therefore fifteen heads, not 842 rows.

## What still has to happen before any of this is a claim

Each row needs its source **read**, not assumed. The pattern of failure
this project has already met once is a citation that sounded right and
pointed at the wrong book, caught in review and publicly retracted. So:

1. Confirm the work, edition and the page or section where the line
   appears, from a scan or a reference work -- not from a summary.
2. Decide the relation honestly per row. `analysed-in` is not
   `invented-by`: Greco and Polerio recorded lines that were already
   played, and the Ruy López carries its author's name because he wrote
   the book, not because he invented 3.Bb5.
3. Grade it. A first-hand reading of the treatise or an entry in a
   reference encyclopaedia is `verified`; anything thinner is
   `attested`, and says so.
4. Only then build a manifest, dry-run it, and put it up for a GO.

Fourteen heads is a week of careful reading, not an afternoon. That is
the correct pace for claims about people.
